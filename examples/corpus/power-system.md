# Power System

The station is powered by a hybrid micro-hydro, battery, solar, and diesel system. The
design principle is simple: store as much energy as possible in the battery bank, use the
hydro turbine whenever Kestrel Creek is flowing, and treat diesel as the winter backup
rather than the default.

## Micro-hydro and battery bank

Turbine A sits in a concrete forebay on Kestrel Creek, about 300 metres uphill from the
powerhouse. Its rated output is 12 kilowatts, and it supplies the station whenever creek
flow is above 40 litres per second, normally from mid-May through mid-October. In winter
the intake freezes and the turbine is drained and locked out.

The battery bank is a 48-volt lithium iron phosphate system rated at 1,200 amp-hours, or
roughly 57 kilowatt-hours nominal (about 40 kilowatt-hours usable within the 20-90 percent
window). It buffers the variable hydro output, carries
the station through short outages, and absorbs surplus solar power from the 3.2 kilowatt
roof array on Polar Lab, installed in 2023. The bank is kept between 20 and 90 percent
state of charge to protect cycle life.

## Winter diesel operation

From November to April the station runs on diesel generators. Gen-1 is a 20 kilowatt unit
and carries the main load; Gen-2 is an 8 kilowatt unit used for light loads, maintenance
days, and as a backup. In deep winter the generators typically run four to six hours per
day, charging the battery bank and heating domestic water, while the battery covers the
quiet hours. The station burns about 2,400 litres of diesel over a winter season. Fuel is
stored in a 6,000-litre bunded tank beside the powerhouse, and the tank is never allowed
below 40 percent between ship deliveries.

Waste heat from the generator jacket is recovered into the lodge heating loop. When the
outside temperature drops below minus 25 degrees, the engineer runs the generator on a
longer schedule so that the lodge, Polar Lab, and the water lines stay above freezing.

## Power emergencies and load shedding

The FjordPower controller monitors voltage, frequency, and battery state. If the battery
falls below 20 percent and no generator is online, the controller enters emergency mode and
sheds loads in three tiers. Tier 1, which is never shed, covers the Polar Lab freezers, the
radio room, the satellite link, the infirmary, and the fuel tank heater. Tier 2 covers
lodge heating and the workshop. Tier 3 covers non-essential lighting, laundry, and the
sauna.

A dedicated emergency generator, EG-5, is a 5 kilowatt unit in the radio hut. It starts
automatically within 30 seconds if both main generators fail, and it runs only the Tier 1
circuits on a dedicated fuel line. The station also keeps a manual changeover panel so the
engineer can connect EG-5 to the main distribution board if the automatic transfer switch
fails. In a total power emergency, the station manager radios the regional rescue service
and all field travel is suspended until power and communications are restored.
