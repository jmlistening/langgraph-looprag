# Wildlife Monitoring

The station's wildlife program combines a permanent acoustic grid with a small set of
visual bird-count plots. The aim is to measure seasonal presence and activity, not to
handle animals. All observations are non-invasive, and the programme is reviewed each
spring by the consortium's animal-welfare panel.

## Acoustic sensor grid

Twenty-four autonomous acoustic sensors, labelled AC-01 through AC-24, are deployed around
the fjord shore and on the lower slopes of the Wall. Each unit records ten minutes at the
start of every hour, driven by a temperature-compensated clock, and stores audio locally on
a 128-gigabyte SD card. The sensors send a short health ping over VHF once a day; audio is
not transmitted. Technicians collect the cards weekly during summer and every two weeks in
winter, replacing them with pre-formatted cards so that recording never pauses.

The grid targets five focal species: black-legged kittiwake, Atlantic puffin, Arctic tern,
snow bunting, and the harbour seal. Analysts run a bird-call classifier over the recordings
and manually review a random ten percent sample each month to estimate classifier error.

## Bird count plots

Six marked plots along the fjord are counted on foot. Counts take place twice weekly from
15 May to 31 August, weather permitting, and are cancelled when wind exceeds 12 metres per
second or visibility falls below 500 metres. Each count lasts twenty minutes and records
species, behaviour, and distance band. Two observers work together during the first week of
the season to keep distance estimates consistent.

## Data cadence and storage

Acoustic health pings are logged daily, audio cards are collected weekly in summer, and
bird-count sheets are entered into the station database within 48 hours. The data manager
checks for gaps every Friday. Raw files are mirrored to two drives kept in separate
buildings, and the monthly archive is uploaded to the consortium server over the satellite
link when bandwidth allows.

## Ethical handling

Visitors may observe wildlife only from a distance of at least 300 metres from birds and
marine mammals. Playback calls are prohibited during the nesting season, which runs from
15 May to 31 July. Injured animals are not handled by station staff; the field safety
officer contacts the regional wildlife authority. Drones are prohibited within five
kilometres of the station and along the entire fjord shore, without exception. Researchers
who need close-approach permits must obtain them through the consortium, not through the
station manager.
