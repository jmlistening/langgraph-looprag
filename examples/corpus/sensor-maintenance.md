# Sensor Maintenance

The station's instruments are maintained on a fixed schedule so that gaps and calibration
drifts are easy to separate from real environmental change. The power and facilities
engineer owns the schedule; the data manager signs off every completed service in the
maintenance log.

## Calibration schedule

Acoustic sensors AC-01 to AC-24 are checked and calibrated every 90 days. The check uses a
portable reference tone generator and a sound-level meter, and the result is recorded in
the unit's service history. Cup anemometers on the meteorological mast are calibrated every
six months, with a field comparison against the handheld reference. Water-quality probes
are calibrated monthly during the open-water season and stored wet over winter.

The hydro intake flow meter is verified at the start and end of each turbine season. If a
probe drifts more than five percent from its reference, it is replaced rather than adjusted
in the field.

## Antifouling and cleaning

The two intake screens on Kestrel Creek are brushed and inspected every 14 days while the
turbine is running, because algae and fine sediment quickly reduce flow. A copper-free
antifouling coating is renewed at the start of each season. Sensor lenses, solar panels,
and the satellite dome are cleaned weekly; in blowing snow the dome may need cleaning
daily. Visitors are not asked to climb the mast or open sensor housings.

## Firmware and spares

Firmware updates are released quarterly and installed in two stages: one test unit first,
then the rest of the grid after a week of stable operation. Every update keeps the previous
image on the device so that a failed unit can be rolled back on site. Updates are never
installed during the nesting season.

The spare-parts store holds two hydro bearings, eight acoustic sensor membranes, one
generator controller, four SD-card readers, and a complete spare sensor unit. Stock levels
are checked at the start of every month and before the winter resupply. Items used from the
store must be logged with the date and the instrument they were fitted to.

## Maintenance records

Each service entry records the instrument identifier, date, technician, work performed,
reference standard used, and any parts consumed. The log is kept in the station database
and mirrored to the data manager's archive. An instrument with an open fault is marked as
unreliable in the public dataset until it is repaired and recalibrated.
