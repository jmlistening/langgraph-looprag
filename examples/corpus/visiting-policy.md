# Visiting Policy

Northwind Fjord Field Station is not a tourist destination. Visits are limited to people
with a research, maintenance, or official reason, and every visit needs a written permit
approved by the station manager at least 60 days before arrival. The cap is twelve visitors
on station at any one time, including researchers who arrive on the same flight.

## Permits and seasonal access

Permit applications state the purpose, dates, required lab space, field sites, and the name
of the sponsoring programme. The station manager consults the safety officer and the power
engineer before approving. Permits are refused when the requested period overlaps a
transition with no scheduled transport, or when the summer bed capacity is already full.

Scheduled access follows the seasons. From January to April, visitors arrive on the
chartered ski plane that lands on the fjord ice twice a week. From June to September,
visitors arrive on the supply ship MV Nordlys or on chartered boats from the outer coast.
May and the second half of October have no scheduled transport, and no visitor permits are
issued for those windows except for emergency maintenance.

## Safety briefing and field rules

Every visitor completes a 90-minute safety briefing before leaving the marked yard. The
briefing covers weather, sea ice, radio procedure, polar bear awareness, and the station's
power emergency rules. Visitors travel in pairs beyond the yard, carry a VHF radio, and
check in with the radio room every two hours. Groups that miss two consecutive check-ins
trigger a search.

Drones are prohibited within five kilometres of the station and anywhere along the fjord
shore. This protects the acoustic wildlife programme and avoids disturbing nesting birds.
Firearms are stored in the locked cabinet in the radio hut and are carried only by staff
who have completed the station's deterrent training. Alcohol is limited to the common room,
and fieldwork is never permitted after drinking.

## Departure and reporting

Visitors return borrowed field gear, submit a short trip report, and hand their data to the
data manager before departure. The trip report includes any wildlife observations, equipment
faults, and near misses. Deliberate breaches of the wildlife distance rule can end a visit
early and are reported to the consortium's ethics panel.
